## Where last-minute discounts actually are right now

A "last minute deal" comes from one of three places, and knowing which one you're shopping changes what you should expect to pay.

The first is the deals section of the big online travel agencies. Expedia, for example, runs ongoing flash sales advertised at up to 50% off, plus a last-minute section covering eligible hotels, vacation rentals and flights — these are limited-time clearances, so inventory and pricing shift constantly. Its One Key loyalty program (free) also unlocks member prices: 10% or more off at 500,000+ hotels at the base tier.

The second is dedicated package sellers. Sites like lastminute.com, Funjet, Apple Vacations and Costco Travel build their business around unsold hotel-and-flight inventory close to the departure date. Airline vacation arms — United Vacations, JetBlue Vacations and similar — do the same thing with their own seat inventory.

The third is the hotels themselves. IHG brands (Holiday Inn, Crowne Plaza, InterContinental and the rest) occasionally show direct-booking promos, and resort chains discount unsold rooms in the final weeks before arrival.

Here's the catch with all three: the advertised discount is already priced in. You can't negotiate it further. What you *can* do is take a cut of the commission the booking site earns on your reservation — which is exactly what a cash-back portal does.

## Stacking: the part that turns 7% into more

Cash back stacks with the sale price by default — the portal pays you on your post-discount total. But two more layers can go on top.

Promo codes come first. TopCashback explicitly allows its cash back to be combined with the promo codes and deals shown on the booking site itself. The cash back is calculated on the discounted price (item price, excluding taxes and fees), so a working code slightly shrinks the cash-back amount — but it does not disqualify the order. Applying a 20% hotel promo code and still earning 7% back on the remainder is fully allowed.

Credit card rewards come second. Paying with a rewards card earns you whatever the card normally pays on travel, completely independent of the portal. And card-linked offers — like the Chase Offers that have recently appeared for Viator and Sixt — stack on top of portal cash back as well, per travel-deal coverage of those promotions.

Roughly speaking, then: sale discount at checkout + portal cash back a few weeks later + credit card rewards. On a $1,000 booking, a 7% portal rate plus a 2%-back card is around $90 of combined return on money you were spending regardless.

## The fine print that actually decides whether you get paid

This is where most of the lost cash back happens, and none of it is buried deep — it's on the merchant terms page, where almost nobody looks.

- **Start with an empty cart.** To be eligible for cash back on Expedia bookings, your purchase must begin with an empty shopping cart. If you've been browsing hotels for an hour and finally click through from the portal with items already saved, that booking can come back untracked.
- **Don't use Safari for this retailer.** TopCashback's own Expedia page states plainly that with the Safari browser it cannot guarantee cash back will track, and recommends Chrome or another browser instead. One browser setting quietly voids the whole exercise.
- **Cash back tracks at $0 until your stay is complete.** For hotel bookings, the transaction shows as $0 pending when you book, and the correct value is only added after you've completed the stay. Don't panic when you see a zero — but do keep your confirmation email in case you need to file a claim later.
- **The claim window is 100 days.** If cash back fails to track automatically, you need to submit a Missing Cash Back Claim within 100 days of your order for Expedia bookings. Claims filed after that window generally don't get honored.
- **Cash back excludes taxes and fees.** The percentage applies to the item price only, and the total cash back can't exceed your purchase amount.

On the payout side: once cash back moves from pending to payable — which can take a few weeks to a couple of months depending on the retailer — you can withdraw to PayPal, direct to your bank, or as a gift card (gift card redemptions sometimes carry a small bonus). Unlike portals that pay out on a fixed quarterly schedule, TopCashback lets you cash out whenever your balance is payable.
